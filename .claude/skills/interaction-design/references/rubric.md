# Rubric

Questions for evaluating interaction quality — someone else's work or your own before handing it off. Walk it as a person doing a real task end to end, not as a tour of features. For a full audit, give each question a verdict (holds, weak, missing, or not relevant and why); for anything weak or missing, say what the person experiences, which fundamental it breaks, and what would fix it. Order findings by frequency × impact × how hard the harm is to undo.

1. **Effort.** Does every step serve the person's goal? What could be removed, defaulted or inferred?
2. **System image.** Can the person tell what's possible and predict what will happen? Does the same thing always look and behave the same, and different things look different?
3. **Convention.** Where does it depart from what people already know, and is each departure worth its learning cost?
4. **Execution and evaluation.** Is the next action visible where the person is looking? Is every result perceivable there, and can they tell whether it worked?
5. **Time.** Does each response fit how long the system takes, on the conditions people actually have? Does each motion carry information, with a reduced-motion equivalent?
6. **Repeated use.** Is the hundredth use fast and safe? Do newcomer aids and expert accelerators stay out of the common path's way? Are modes visible?
7. **Reversibility and weight.** Can actions be undone? Is friction proportional to stakes and inversely to frequency? Do the irreversible ones state their consequence?
8. **Error.** Are likely slips and mistakes prevented? When something fails, does the person learn what happened, keep their work, and see the way forward?
9. **States.** Has each condition that can vary — data, time, connection, access, content, other people — been reasoned through, with behavior decided for reachable combinations that change the action, feedback or recovery?
10. **Directness.** Are controls close in space and time to what they change? Are precise, repeated and bulk tasks served by symbolic paths?
11. **Ability.** Can the core task be completed through more than one channel and input — keyboard alone, screen reader, magnification, reduced motion? Does it meet the current WCAG minimum?
12. **Truth.** Does feedback reflect what actually happened? Does any pattern work against the person's goal?
13. **Meaning.** Do commitment moments carry appropriate weight, and does each flow end with a clear resolution?
14. **Evidence.** Which judgments rest on observed behavior and which on assumption — and what would test the assumptions?

For a focused change, use the questions that could change the interaction. Record actionable findings and their evidence in the existing review.
