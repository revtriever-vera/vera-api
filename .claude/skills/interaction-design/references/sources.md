# Sources

Where the fundamentals come from, and how much weight their claims bear.

## Lineage

| Source | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (rev. 2013) | Mental models, system image, affordances and signifiers, mapping, constraints, the seven stages and two gulfs, slips and mistakes |
| Don Norman, *Emotional Design* (2004) | Visceral, behavioral and reflective levels |
| Alan Cooper et al., *About Face* (4th ed., 2014) | Goal-directed design, excise, perpetual intermediates |
| Jef Raskin, *The Humane Interface* (2000) | Habituation, modes and quasi-modes, undo over confirmation |
| James Reason, *Human Error* (1990) | Error taxonomy |
| Ben Shneiderman, [“Direct Manipulation: A Step Beyond Programming Languages”](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.1983.1654471), *IEEE Computer* (1983) | Direct manipulation; syntactic/semantic model |
| Michel Beaudouin-Lafon, [“Instrumental Interaction”](https://doi.org/10.1145/332040.332473), CHI 2000 | Instruments, indirection, integration, compatibility, reification |
| Robert Miller, "Response time in man-computer conversational transactions" (1968); Card, Moran & Newell, *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction* (1983) | Response time thresholds; keystroke-level model |
| Albert Michotte, *The Perception of Causality* (1946) | Perceived causality in motion |
| Paul Fitts (1954); Hick (1952) and Hyman (1953) | Target acquisition and choice reaction time |
| Brenda Laurel, *Computers as Theatre* (1991) | Interaction as dramatic structure |
| Microsoft, *Inclusive Design* toolkit | Permanent, temporary and situational limitation |
| W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (current version) | The shared accessibility standard |
| Harry Brignull, *Deceptive Patterns* (2023) | Catalog of manipulative interface patterns |

## How much weight the claims bear

- **Perceptual and motor findings** (response thresholds, Fitts's law, change blindness, perceived causality) rest on decades of controlled research and are robust; applying them to a specific design still takes judgment.
- **Design frameworks** (goal-directed design, direct manipulation, instrumental interaction, emotional design) are well-grounded theories and practitioner syntheses, useful for reasoning and comparison, not formulas that produce a correct design.
- **Laws applied as rules** mislead: Hick's law is often quoted to justify removing options people need, and the keystroke-level model ignores learning, error and satisfaction.
- **Transfer to agents.** These ideas describe people using interfaces. An agent designing without watching people use the product produces hypotheses about behavior; observation by people with access confirms or corrects them.
