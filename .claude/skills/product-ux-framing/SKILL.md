# Product UX Framing

Find the product decision that is still open. Check the issue, PRD or product owner’s direction for decisions already made. Carry settled scope into planning. If the remaining question is how a person performs an agreed task, use `interaction-design`.

Start from a specific occasion. Who was trying to do what, what did they use, and where did it fall short? Distinguish an observed workaround from a prediction about adoption. For example, a request for a dashboard tells you what someone asked for. A report that they export a list every Friday to find overdue follow-ups gives you a task against which to judge the dashboard.

For a cheap, reversible change, put the intended outcome and a way to check it in the existing card. For a larger bet, compare plausible alternatives, including the current workaround. Identify the assumption most likely to reverse the choice and the cheapest observation that would test it. Show the scope that fits the agreed time or cost.

The framing is ready when a reader can tell what progress would count, why the chosen scope is worth trying, and which evidence or unresolved assumption supports that decision. If the evidence is missing, identify what would settle it.

- When reviewing a proposal, use the [framing rubric](references/rubric.md) at the depth the decision warrants.
- When adoption, terminology or scope changes the choice, consult the relevant section of [foundations](references/foundations.md).
- When a recommendation depends on the strength of a published claim, check its [source and limitations](references/sources.md).
