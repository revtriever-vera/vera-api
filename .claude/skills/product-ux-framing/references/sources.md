## How much weight the claims bear

- **Practitioner evidence, not controlled studies.** There's no comparative research showing that teams using jobs-to-be-done, opportunity trees or object modeling make better decisions than teams that don't. The support is consistent practitioner experience. Present these as disciplined ways of thinking, not as proven science.
- **Vendor statistics.** Success rates quoted by methodology vendors (Strategyn's widely cited figure for ODI, for example) are self-reported and self-selected. Cite them as claims.
- **Anecdotes.** Famous cases such as the milkshake story illustrate an idea; they don't prove it.
- **Disruption theory.** Christensen's disruption claims have been seriously critiqued (Lepore, 2014; King & Baatartogtokh, 2015), which bounds — without voiding — the authority of his less formalized jobs claims.
- **Transfer to agents.** These methods were developed for human teams in contact with users. An agent's framing is a set of hypotheses until people with access to users and data confirm them.
